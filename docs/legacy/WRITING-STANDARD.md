# Writing Standard

## Purpose

Define a consistent writing style for legacy documentation so institutional intent remains clear, precise, and durable over time.

## Scope

Applies to all prose content in docs/legacy, including standards, policies, manuals, decision records, and research summaries.

## Responsibilities

- Authors
  - Write with clarity, evidence, and operational specificity.
- Reviewers
  - Reject ambiguous language and unsupported claims.
- Editorial Steward
  - Enforce style consistency and plain-language quality.

## Process

1. Write in direct, professional language.
2. Use active voice for responsibilities and controls.
3. Prefer explicit terms over broad abstractions.
4. Separate facts, assumptions, and recommendations.
5. Support consequential claims with evidence references.
6. Keep section headings consistent with document templates.
7. Use bullets for procedures and numbered steps for workflows.

## Examples

- Preferred:
  - The Domain Custodian approves policy exceptions within two business days.

- Not preferred:
  - Policy exceptions may be reviewed in due course by relevant stakeholders.

- Preferred:
  - This control prevents undocumented authority delegation.

- Not preferred:
  - This control helps improve governance outcomes.

## Future Evolution

Future upgrades may include:
- Controlled terminology linting against Deepsea Dictionary
- Readability scoring thresholds by document class
- Evidence citation standards by domain criticality
